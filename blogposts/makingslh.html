Published June 10, 2026

In April of 2024 I was rummaging around the internet for any unblocked games websites to play during my free time at school. As most Aussie kids know, Year 10 is the most chill and laid back year of your entire high school experience, so during any spare moments, most people were playing unblocked games on their school issued chromebooks. Our IT admins had blocked most popular games websites, and so we were stuck hopping from one unblocked games site to the next, but the IT admins always caught up within a few days.

Now it was around this time that I had discovered HTML and I was building some simple websites with it, and while looking through a list of HTML tags I noticed the embed tag. I further noticed that sites like Crazy Games had an embed button, where you could copy a piece of code into a HTML page to put a game on your website. After a few hours of going through Crazy Games and picking out a few of my personal favourite games, I had my own personal unblocked games website. I named it spacepirate's localhost, since I was using localhost:3000 at home to develop it. The name stuck, even though it was no longer locally hosted.

One month later, after the school holidays, my classmates found that all unblocked games websites, or really any page with the words 'unblocked games' had been blocked. Apparently the IT department had finally figured out how to mass filter websites. At the time I was using the online coding platform Replit, (which is now a vibe coding ai slop factory) and it provided me a developer URL with which to test my site in a production environment. It wasn't actually hosted anywhere, and you had to have the Replit project running, but it was the only way to use the site.

Eventually people started noticing that I wasn't getting any work done in class and wondered how I was playing unblocked games. I explained that I made my own website, and they asked how they could get onto it. I figured out that I needed to actually host the website somewhere for real, not just use a developer page thing. So I looked into hosting services. Github pages wouldn't work since it used the .io extension and the admins had blocked that ages ago. Cloudflare was too finicky and it took way too long to upload all the files (since my site was also a place to archive my favourite flash games). But then I found Vercel.

It was a bit of a learning curve to switch from Replit's AI powered developer environment to VS Code, but it was worth it to have my own domain, albeit with a .vercel.app extension at the end of it. So I connected my Github repository to the Vercel project and started giving out the URL. As most programmers know, there is no better feeling than seeing your creation get used by people, and it was amazing seeing the analytics page and all the people that were enjoying my website. I opened some Google Forms so people could submit requests for games to be added, and feedback forms to see how I could improve the site. Over the course of about 4 months, I made several major revisions, and I eventually hit 100 total games on the website.

Obviously the IT department wasn't very happy about my unblocked games website, so they blocked the URL. But, Vercel allows you to create as many domains as you want for your website, as long as they're unique and have the .vercel.app extension at the end of it. So for another 8 months, my site remained up.

All good things must come to an end, though. I went into Year 11, and we were now allowed to use our own personal devices for schoolwork. People stopped using the website, I stopped recieving feedback, and thus I stopped adding new features. Nowadays I only use it to play some old flash games and store some important files like ASCII tables and wallpapers without having to log into google.
